# Style: Drama Serial

Prestige TV in a vertical frame. Letterboxed black, antique gold serif titles, cream text, film grain, slow push-ins and gentle fades, like the title sequence of a serious drama serial. Works as solemn storytelling, or as deadpan comedy when loud moments smash-cut into the calm.

## Use it for
- Story-driven content: founder stories, origin stories, "the day everything broke" narratives.
- Pop-culture and drama parodies, especially Pakistani drama audiences who know the title-card language.
- Credits-style reveals of people, players, customers or a team ("starring...").

## Avoid it for
- Dense data or step-by-step explainers; the slow pacing fights clarity.
- Fast product demos where viewers need to see UI quickly.

## Color tokens
| Role | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Letterbox | #000000 | Bars at top and bottom |
| Background | #12100D | Inside the letterbox (warm near-black) |
| Primary text | #E8E2D0 | Body, names, supporting lines (cream) |
| Secondary text | #B8B2A2 | Small-caps labels and credits detail |
| Accent | #C9A24B | Hero numbers, rules, the most important title (antique gold) |

Accent rule: gold is used for the hero element and thin decorative rules only. Never body text.

## Type
- Display: Cormorant Garamond 500 and 700, plus italic for taglines.
- Text: Inter 500 and 600, used only for small-caps labels and credit details.
- Labels: all caps, letter spacing 0.28 to 0.32em, secondary color.
- Numbers: Cormorant lining figures for hero numbers; Inter tabular for small counts.

## Layout
- Centered composition, unlike the other styles.
- Letterbox bars of about 180px at 1080x1920 sit inside the platform safe zones, so the visible picture stays clean.
- Lots of air around each line; one line or one name on screen at a time for title cards.

## Texture
- Film grain at 4 to 6 percent over everything, plus a gentle vignette. This is the style's signature texture.
- A very slight warm grade on any footage to match the gold and cream.

## Footage
- Shown full-bleed inside the letterbox, slightly darkened and warm-graded, with a slow 3 percent push-in.
- For comedy: footage cuts in hard and loud, at full brightness and with original sound, then cuts back to calm.

## Signature moves
- Title card: a small-caps label fades in, a thin gold rule draws outward from the center in 400 ms, then the serif title fades up over 600 ms.
- Slow push-in: every scene drifts in by 3 percent over its full duration; nothing is ever static.
- Credits reveal: names appear one at a time, centered, with a small-caps detail beneath ("2,555 SLAPS"), cross-fading over 400 ms.
- Smash cut: for comedy, silence and stillness for half a second, then a hard cut to the loudest moment of footage with full sound, then a hard cut back to the calm frame.
- Dip to black: scene changes dip to black over 300 ms rather than cutting, except for smash cuts.

## Music
- Slow orchestral or piano drama score, sparse and emotional, around 70 BPM. Silence before smash cuts.

## Pacing note
- This style runs slower than the defaults in editing-rules.md: holds may stretch to 2.5 seconds after a line finishes, except in the first two seconds, which still follow the hook rules.
